# Vision feature and commercial research v0.1

**Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Status:** Pre-Opus research synthesis; pricing strategy deliberately not decided  
**Scope:** ZoomText Magnifier / Magnifier-Reader, Dolphin SuperNova Magnifier family, low-vision workplace use, current Vision baseline, and implications for a 2-4 monitor Vision product.

## 1. Research question

The purpose is not to reproduce ZoomText or SuperNova feature-for-feature. The purpose is to identify the smallest defensible Vision product that captures a large share of the value required by users who still work visually, while remaining materially simpler and potentially much cheaper.

The technical and commercial questions are linked:

1. What user problem does each incumbent capability solve?
2. Is it central to low-vision work or peripheral/niche?
3. Does Vision already implement some of it?
4. How difficult would an independent implementation be?
5. What compatibility/support burden does it create?
6. Does it materially affect purchase, assessment, dealer demonstration, workplace deployment, or retention?

This is black-box / clean-room product research. Public documentation and ordinary product behaviour may be studied. No decompilation, binary inspection, licensing bypass or copying of proprietary implementation is required or intended.

## 2. Product boundary: low vision first

Both incumbent families establish a useful boundary. Freedom Scientific describes ZoomText Magnifier as visual magnification/enhancement with no speech, and Magnifier/Reader as the same visual product plus speech and reading tools; it explicitly says Magnifier/Reader is still primarily for low-vision users and points users who need full screen reading toward Fusion/JAWS. Dolphin similarly separates SuperNova Magnifier, Magnifier & Speech, and Magnifier & Screen Reader.

This strongly supports an initial Vision target of **people with useful functional vision who primarily work visually**. Full blind-user screen-reader semantics, Braille, deep keyboard exploration and comparable JAWS/SuperNova Screen Reader behaviour should not be assumed part of the first Vision product.

- https://support.freedomscientific.com/Services/TrainingAndCertification/ZoomTextFAQs

## 3. User-value evidence

External user evidence supports focusing on interaction quality rather than sheer feature count. In a workplace study of 314 employed people with blindness or low vision, 59.1% identified access to particular software, websites or documents as a challenge. For people with some functional vision, screen magnification was the most commonly used computer-access AT in the cited workplace research.

A separate Office-productivity study with ten experienced low-vision magnifier users found substantial time and effort spent panning back and forth, repeatedly changing zoom, and keeping the text caret visible while editing. The problem was not lack of maximum zoom; it was maintaining context and efficiently reaching commands and feedback while only part of the interface was visible.

## 5. First feature-priority conclusion

The research does **not** support a feature-parity strategy. It supports a concentrated first product built around:

- high-quality magnification and text rendering;
- pointer, caret and focus tracking;
- predictable viewport movement and orientation recovery;
- colour/contrast and simple visual highlighting;
- robust application compatibility;
- a genuinely good 2-4 monitor model, including monitor-boundary behaviour;
- simple configuration/persistence;
- dependable install/update/diagnostics.

The strongest candidates for the next layer are simple app-specific profiles, Freeze/Hooked-Area-like pinned regions, collaboration/presentation modes, and possibly basic speech. Full screen reading, Braille, camera/whiteboard and remote-enterprise parity should not contaminate the first architecture unless evidence changes the product boundary.

### 6.2 Maintenance and support are part of the product

The incumbents are not merely charging for executable code. Dolphin states that a new purchase includes a 12-month Software Maintenance Agreement in its direct sales flow. Its SMA includes major upgrades, minor updates, UK telephone/remote/email/chat support and newer AI assistance. SuperNova v25 material frames ongoing SMA value around remaining compatible with changing workplace applications.

Freedom Scientific similarly sells subscription and perpetual business licences, with updates during subscriptions and SMA on perpetual products. Its 2026 work includes Windows 11 changes, graphics-adapter changes, published applications and multiple-4K-monitor compatibility.

Commercial implication: compatibility maintenance and support are product features. A cheaper Vision product cannot be evaluated only on the cost of implementing visible controls; it needs a supportable compatibility envelope.

### 6.3 Channel structure

Both vendors use specialist channels. Freedom Scientific sells directly and via local authorised dealers. Dolphin actively routes international sales through trained dealers/resellers, and its purchase flow asks customers to select a dealer to provide support. Dolphin Professional and Enterprise licences add organisational deployment capabilities such as multi-user licensing, MSI distribution, virtual/remote environments and unattended installation.

This means dealers are not simply e-commerce affiliates. They can contribute assessment, product selection, installation, configuration, training and ongoing support. Exact dealer gross-margin percentages are not public in the sources reviewed. Public procurement discounts and charity referral discounts are not reliable substitutes for dealer margin and should not be used to invent a margin model.

Dealer economics therefore remain a primary interview question for the later commercial phase.

### 6.4 Funding and willingness-to-pay context

The UK workplace market is partly mediated by assessment and public funding rather than ordinary consumer willingness to pay. In the revised Access to Work statistics for the financial year ending March 2025:

- 3,820 customers receiving an Access to Work Element payment had Difficulty in seeing as the primary medical condition;
- that group accounted for GBP 38.2m of Element expenditure;
- average annual Element payment for that group was GBP 9,300;
- across all Access to Work Element recipients, 59% were associated with large employers.

These figures are not a screen-magnifier market size. They include many forms of support. They do show that assistive software is bought inside a much larger support bundle where assessment, equipment, training, support workers and other adjustments can dwarf software licence cost.

### 6.5 Market-size caution

RNIB reports more than two million people living with sight loss in the UK, but nearly 80% are 65 or older. The whole sight-loss population is therefore a poor proxy for an addressable Windows workplace magnifier market. Better near-term demand proxies are working-age users with functional vision, workplace-assessment/funding volumes, education, existing specialist-software users and dealer customer bases.

No defensible public figure for current ZoomText or SuperNova installed base or UK market share was found. Any market-share target would therefore be false precision before dealer/vendor/customer research.

Source:
- https://www.rnib.org.uk/professionals/research-and-data/key-information-and-statistics-on-sight-loss-in-the-uk/

### 6.6 Feature value and commercial value are not identical

The commercial importance of a feature depends on more than end-user delight. Four categories matter:

1. **Daily-use value** - does the user rely on it constantly? Tracking, text clarity, orientation, colour and viewport behaviour belong here.
2. **Assessment / demonstration value** - does the capability make the product obviously credible during an AT assessment or dealer demo? Multi-monitor behaviour, reliable Office/browser tracking and visible enhancement controls matter here.
3. **Support-cost value** - does the feature or architecture reduce calls, configuration problems and regressions? Diagnostics, compatibility testing, safe defaults, updates and simple profiles matter here.
4. **Procurement / organisational value** - does it unlock employers, education or managed estates? MSI/unattended deployment, shared-machine behaviour, remote environments and support commitments matter here, but can be deferred by choosing an initial market boundary.

This means a GBP 100-class Vision cannot simply remove support and compatibility work. The plausible disruption is a smaller, clearer capability set with lower engineering and training complexity, not a fragile copy of an incumbent magnifier.

## 7. Switching difficulty and competitive entry

Technical implementation is only one barrier. Incumbents benefit from user muscle memory, AT-assessor familiarity, trained dealer channels, workplace procurement history, established support, app-compatibility knowledge and maintenance programmes. Those make immediate replacement of the incumbent installed base difficult even if Vision is cheaper.

However, those same incumbents expose substantial complexity. A focused product can plausibly compete where the user mainly needs visual access rather than screen-reader functionality, especially if Vision is simpler to learn, materially cheaper, and excellent on modern multi-monitor Windows setups.

The practical entry hypothesis is therefore not: replace every ZoomText/SuperNova user. It is: identify a magnification-focused cohort for whom the incumbent suite is more complex and expensive than necessary, then prove that Vision covers their recurring tasks with lower friction.

## 9. Questions the hands-on tests must answer

- At 2x, 3x and 4x, how materially better are xFont/True Fonts than Vision raster scaling in Edge, Word, Excel and Outlook?
- How do ZoomText and SuperNova arbitrate pointer, caret and focus when signals compete?
- How much smoothing versus latency do their viewport movements introduce?
- What happens exactly when the pointer approaches and crosses a monitor boundary?
- Which multi-monitor modes remain understandable after 10-15 minutes of use, and which feel like legacy complexity?
- How useful are Freeze View / Hooked Areas in real Office tasks?
- How quickly can an experienced Windows user discover and configure the core magnifier without training?
- What CPU/GPU/memory overhead appears on the same Dell workload?

## 10. Pre-Opus research conclusion

The evidence currently supports building Vision as a **focused low-vision magnification product, not a reduced screen reader**. Its strongest potential is not merely cheaper zoom. It is a simpler visual-access product with high-quality tracking and orientation plus a modern 2-4 monitor model.

The hardest parts are unlikely to be zoom arithmetic or UI controls. They are:

- text quality at higher magnification;
- application compatibility and long-tail tracking behaviour;
- generalising the current one-source/one-detail runtime into a coherent 2-4 monitor model;
- keeping the product simple while preserving the few incumbent behaviours users depend on;
- sustaining compatibility/support cheaply enough that a disruptive price remains economically viable.

The detailed Vision development plan should therefore optimise architecture for those risks and explicitly defer expensive breadth until user and dealer evidence justifies it.

## 11. Hands-on correction after incumbent feature sweep

The initial matrix understated the importance and breadth of visual locator/enhancement features. Hands-on SuperNova 25.05 and current ZoomText documentation show that tracking and visual emphasis are separate product layers: one determines what point of interest to follow, while the other makes that point of interest easy to see.

This section supersedes the earlier cautious treatment of pointer/caret/focus enhancements where the two conflict.

### 11.1 SuperNova 25.05 installed feature surface

The installed Magnifier edition exposes the following Visual-menu families on the Dell bench:

- Full Screen, Fixed Window, Magnifying Glass, Resizing Glass and four Split Screen orientations;
- 24+ colour schemes plus custom configuration;
- Highlighting ON/OFF, seven preset highlight schemes and custom/settings;
- Mouse Pointers configuration;
- Doc Reader settings;
- Hooked Areas;
- Connect and View and Whiteboard;
- Multiple Monitors;
- Visual Advanced Options.

Outside the Visual menu it also exposes Situation/Application Settings, import/export of settings, Item Finder, keyboard/hotkey settings, touchscreen settings, EasyReader, media/news/podcast functions and support/update tooling.

### 11.2 Visual highlighting is a core capability, not cosmetic polish

SuperNova 25.05 exposes seven installed presets: Default, Large Red, Large Red with Mouse, Large Blue, Large Blue with Mouse, Large Yellow and Large Yellow with Mouse.
Its custom model can target mouse, text cursor, line and Windows focus, with configurable shape, colour, size, transparency and display timing.
The Dell bench captured representative Large Red and Large Yellow variants against the same caret fixture.

## 12. Authoritative v0.2 corrections after hands-on incumbent research

This section supersedes earlier prioritisation where there is a conflict. Full evidence is in `2026-09-30__gpt-5.6-sol__incumbent_hands_on_validation_v0.2.md` and `lab/incumbent-research/supernova_25_05_installed_menu_tree.txt`.

### Visual locators move into first-product core

Tracking and visual emphasis are separate problems. Vision already has pointer/caret/focus evidence and basic locator overlays. Both incumbents devote first-class product surface to making those targets visually obvious.

SuperNova exposes seven installed highlight presets plus custom mouse, cursor, line and focus highlighting. ZoomText separately exposes Pointer, Cursor and Focus enhancement systems.

Recommendation: ship a small coherent set of high-visibility pointer/caret/focus presets and independent on/off controls in the first serious Vision product. Do not copy the incumbents' full theme editors or hundreds of pointer images initially.

Architecture implication: introduce an explicit visual-overlay policy layer. It consumes pointer/caret/focus evidence but is separate from POI arbitration and viewport ownership. It should support per-target style and per-view/per-monitor scope.

### View geometry moves from medium to medium-high importance

SuperNova exposes eight geometries. ZoomText exposes Full, Overlay, Lens, Line and Docked views on single-monitor setups, plus dedicated extended-desktop modes. Full-screen remains the dominant incumbent default, but alternative views solve a different user problem: preserving unmagnified context while inspecting detail.

Recommendation: do not reproduce every incumbent geometry. Keep Full plus at least one contextual/lens/fixed-window mode as an explicit first-product validation target. Context + Detail may cover much of this on multi-monitor systems but does not remove the one-monitor need.

### Text and image quality remain architecture gates

Current Dolphin documentation states that SuperNova TrueFonts is the default where supported and uses real font substitution. In hardware-accelerated browsers such as Edge, Chrome and Firefox it falls back to Standard smoothing. ZoomText xFont is likewise on by default in supported Office 365 applications.

This means the browser comparison bench cannot settle Vision's text-quality architecture. A supported Office application is the correct comparison environment. A deterministic Word fixture and SuperNova capture were prepared, but exact automated product-state matching was not reliable enough to claim a controlled Vision-vs-TrueFonts result.

Recommendation: Opus should treat an Office text-quality experiment as a pre-implementation decision gate, not assume either that raster scaling is sufficient or that semantic text re-rendering is necessary.

### Multi-monitor behaviour is a product subsystem

SuperNova's current model includes Span, side-by-side applications, Presentation Mode, adjustable Mouse Buffer, switch-monitor hotkey, independent magnification levels and independent colour schemes. Visual highlighting can also be scoped to the current monitor or all monitors.

ZoomText's extended-desktop modes include Span, Clone, Zoom with 1x, MultiView Local and MultiView Global, while newer ZoomText work also includes Single Desktop and per-display screen-enhancement scope.

Recommendation: Vision's advantage should not be "supports several monitors". It should be a simpler and more coherent monitor-role/view model that covers the recurring jobs with fewer concepts.

### Application settings rise in commercial importance

Both incumbent families preserve application-specific behaviour/settings. This is not merely power-user customisation: it reduces repeated configuration and support burden when different workplace applications need different magnification, colour, tracking or enhancement behaviour.

Recommendation: preserve an architectural seam for app-specific settings and plan a simple implementation after the generic view model is stable.
