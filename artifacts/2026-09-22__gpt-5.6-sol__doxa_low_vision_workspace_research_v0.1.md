# Doxa low-vision workspace research v0.1

**Date:** 2026-09-22
**Project:** Visual / Doxa
**Status:** initial research framing and working hypotheses

## Product scope

The primary product objective is not to create a broadly distributed standalone magnifier. It is to make Doxa a strong low-vision workstation.

Visual is the enabling software companion and current engineering test bed. Until a Doxa Pro 3 unit is available, continue validating the core on the existing two-monitor setup. In parallel, research how the physical Doxa layout should be used by people with low vision.

Installer, LLM support and general distribution are secondary until the Doxa usage model is better understood.

## Physical Doxa starting point

The Doxa Pro 3 provides three 15.6-inch Full-HD IPS panels on an elevated, height-adjustable stand. Product imagery shows the three Doxa panels arranged as an upper row, with the laptop open below the centre panel. Left and right panels can rotate substantially, allowing a flat/wide arrangement or a more wrapped configuration.

This creates four visually distinct surfaces:

- upper-left Doxa panel;
- upper-centre Doxa panel;
- upper-right Doxa panel;
- lower-centre laptop panel.

The geometry means these surfaces should not automatically be assigned equal roles.

## Evidence relevant to the product hypothesis

Low-vision screen magnification research shows that magnification improves access but reduces visible context. Users differ substantially in preferred magnification mode and often adopt stable preferred screen regions while reading. Viewing distance and display size interact with magnification; some low-vision users naturally work closer to a screen.

Recent low-vision chart-access research reinforces the value of context plus detail: providing contextual elements can improve access and spatial understanding, although adding simultaneous visual information can also increase visual load.

General multi-monitor field research finds that secondary displays are often used for peripheral awareness rather than as equal primary workspaces.

Current ZoomText multi-monitor features and user reports also show demand for:

- one magnified view plus one unmagnified/context view;
- independent/locked views per monitor;
- freeze/reference views;
- preventing a magnifier from treating all displays as one enormous panning surface.

Anecdotal low-vision user reports include a workflow where one monitor is used for normal navigation/context while a second monitor is dedicated to a much more highly magnified detail view.

## Core Doxa research question

What allocation of physical screens, magnification states and application roles gives low-vision users the best combination of:

- readable detail;
- stable orientation/context;
- low visual search burden;
- low head/neck movement;
- fast pointer/caret recovery;
- productivity across real work tasks?

## Important competing hypotheses

### H1 — Upper-centre Detail

Upper-centre Doxa panel is the main dynamic magnified Detail surface.

Possible roles:

- upper-centre: dynamic Detail;
- upper-left: stable Context/Overview;
- upper-right: Reference/Communication;
- laptop: controls, launcher or current application/context.

Rationale: centre upper screen is closest to the user's primary forward gaze and likely minimizes repeated head rotation.

Risk: the Doxa panel may be physically farther away than the laptop; some low-vision users may rely on short viewing distance and prefer the laptop for high-detail reading.

### H2 — Laptop as Close Detail

Laptop panel is the high-magnification Detail surface because it is physically closer, while the upper row preserves larger-scale context and productivity roles.

Possible roles:

- laptop: close Detail / reading;
- upper-centre: main Work/Context;
- upper-left: Reference;
- upper-right: Communication/secondary context.

Rationale: shorter viewing distance increases angular size and may be valuable for users who naturally work close to displays.

Risk: prolonged downward gaze and repeated vertical shifts may be less comfortable; laptop panel is smaller and lower.

### H3 — One Dynamic Screen, Three Stable Screens

Only one screen should move/follow caret/pointer automatically at a time. The other three should be spatially stable.

This is currently the strongest software/product hypothesis because low-vision magnification already reduces spatial context; making several screens pan simultaneously is likely to increase visual load and orientation cost.

### H4 — Wrapped Doxa for Restricted Useful Field

Angling left/right Doxa panels inward may reduce the required head rotation and bring more useful information into the user's effective field of view.

This should be tested against the standard flat/wide arrangement rather than assumed.

## Workflow presets to research

Do not begin with disease labels. Begin with tasks and functional visual needs.

### Reading / writing

- dynamic magnified text/caret Detail;
- stable full-page/document overview;
- reference/source material;
- laptop reserved for controls or close-detail alternative.

### Compare / data entry

- magnified active field or destination;
- stable source document;
- stable application/page context;
- secondary reference or communication.

### Communication + work

- active Work/Detail surface;
- stable meeting/communication surface;
- stable reference/context surface;
- laptop as control/context or close-detail fallback.

### High-magnification / reduced field

- one primary active display;
- side screens optionally dimmed, simplified or activated on demand rather than constantly filled;
- strong keyboard shortcuts and predictable return-to-primary behavior.

## Functional-vision variables to include in research

Different low-vision users may need materially different layouts. Research should record functional needs such as:

- preferred viewing distance;
- preferred magnification;
- usable central vs peripheral field;
- sensitivity to visual clutter;
- ability/willingness to turn head between screens;
- preferred contrast/brightness;
- mouse vs keyboard-heavy workflow;
- need for stable reference information.

Do not assume that more simultaneously visible information is always better.

## Physical parameters to test when Doxa arrives

- flat vs inward-wrapped side panels;
- Doxa height level;
- user distance from the upper row;
- laptop distance and tilt;
- Windows scaling on each panel;
- brightness/contrast uniformity across the three Doxa panels and laptop;
- which panel is easiest to acquire visually after a pointer/caret move;
- head turns per task;
- vertical gaze shifts to/from laptop;
- whether side panels are useful continuously or better as occasional reference surfaces.

## Proposed research method

### Before Doxa arrives

1. Continue two-monitor Visual engineering until the basic magnifier is stable.
2. Review low-vision magnification and multi-monitor literature.
3. Review competitor multi-monitor/freeze/context features.
4. Collect real low-vision user reports about multi-monitor workflows.
5. Define a small set of Doxa workflow prototypes and test questions.
6. Avoid building Doxa-specific software behavior that depends on untested role assumptions.

### Once Doxa arrives

Run staged experiments:

1. Physical geometry only: height, angle, distance, laptop position.
2. Static role layouts without Visual automation.
3. One dynamic magnified Detail screen + stable context screens.
4. Compare upper-centre Detail against laptop-close Detail.
5. Test wrapped vs flat side panels.
6. Test representative tasks: reading/writing, research/reference, forms/data entry, meetings.
7. Only then formalize Doxa-specific defaults/presets in Visual.

## Initial success measures

For each layout/workflow, capture:

- task completion time;
- errors/missed controls;
- number of times the user loses pointer/caret/focus;
- number of unnecessary monitor transitions;
- subjective orientation confidence;
- subjective reading comfort;
- perceived visual load;
- head/neck movement/discomfort;
- preferred layout and why.

## Current recommendation

Until Doxa hardware arrives:

- keep Visual focused on the two-monitor POC and basic tracking/magnification quality;
- treat Doxa low-vision workspace design as a parallel research track;
- do not prioritize installer/LLM/general distribution architecture;
- do not lock in a four-screen role model yet.

The highest-value research question is not 'how do we magnify four monitors?' It is:

> Which screen should move, which screens should remain stable, and what should each physical screen show so that the four-screen Doxa geometry reduces effort rather than increasing visual search?
